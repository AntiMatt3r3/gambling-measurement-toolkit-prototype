Scope, methods and limitations

About this prototype

A public-facing measurement resource built from a structured, auditable research database.

Purpose

The toolkit helps public-health teams, services, commissioners, third-sector organisations and researchers understand what measurement options are available and what is currently known about using them.

This is the first functioning component of a wider Evaluation and Monitoring Toolkit, conceptually supporting the pathway from intervention and evaluation approach through outcomes, measurement and interpretation.

What Website V2 contains

The current public layer contains 42 measurement options across 12 outcome domains. It includes conventional instruments, a diagnostic assessment, a structured behavioural method, direct indicators and a qualitative approach.

Measurement option

A public umbrella term for a reproducible instrument, assessment, method, operational indicator or qualitative framework that can generate information about an outcome. An outcome itself is not automatically a measurement option.

How records entered the dataset

The database began with an auditable source review and was normalised so that named instruments, generic outcomes, behavioural variables, clinician ratings and process information were not treated as equivalent. Twenty-four records were then hardened through targeted curation. A3 added a lighter, breadth-first set to test the wider information architecture.

Evidence and application are different

Measurement-property evidence concerns matters such as validity, reliability, responsiveness and interpretability. A paper that merely uses an option does not establish those properties. Research-provenance-only citations remain in the backend but are not displayed as substantive evidence cards.

Understanding curation depth

Hardened core
Identity, specification, access and a bounded evidence package have undergone detailed curation.
Prototype curated
The record meets a lighter public prototype gate intended to stress-test breadth and architecture.
Curation depth is not a quality judgement. It says how completely the database record has been reviewed, not whether one option is better than another.

“Not reviewed” is not “no evidence”

An empty evidence display means no substantive finding has yet been curated for that exact public record. Evidence may exist outside this bounded exercise. Conversely, an intended evaluation use does not establish that an instrument is responsive to change.

Prototype operational definitions

Several direct indicators are included to demonstrate how behavioural, service and transactional outcomes might be operationalised. They are clearly labelled as prototypes and are not presented as validated or national standards. Their definitions, windows, derivations and data-quality assumptions must travel together.

Rights and access

The resource links to instruments and source material rather than reproducing questionnaire items. Permission to use, reproduce, modify or use commercially are separate questions. Public availability is not treated as reproduction permission.

Health economics

Preference-based utility measures, capability measures, observational associations and mappings are kept conceptually distinct. The prototype does not provide calculators or create new crosswalks.

Conceptual basis and future development

The wider programme is informed by MRC/NIHR guidance on developing and evaluating complex interventions. Later modules may address programme theory, evaluation design, process and implementation evaluation, mixed methods, templates and interpretation. Those modules are not built here.

Version

Public dataset: v4 A3 expanded, generated 2026-09-20. This remains a private proof of concept and should not be treated as a complete or publication-ready repository.
